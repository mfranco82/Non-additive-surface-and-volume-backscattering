# Non-additive surface and volume backscattering

Code accompanying **M. Franco and E. Calzetta, _Non-additive surface and volume backscattering
from a rough interface over a correlated random medium_**.

[![DOI](https://zenodo.org/badge/DOI/10.5281/zenodo.22832455.svg)](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22832455)

The paper computes the first-order incoherent backscattering cross-section of a random rough
surface bounding a half-space whose permittivity fluctuates randomly, in the case where the two
random fields are **correlated**, so that surface and volume scattering are not additive. A
non-orthogonal coordinate transformation

```
z = ζ + h(x) f(ζ)
```

maps the rough boundary onto a flat interface and converts the geometric roughness into volume
sources of the same Helmholtz equation as the dielectric fluctuations, so that both mechanisms
and their interference follow from a single calculation.

This repository contains the symbolic derivation of the first-order sources, the numerical
evaluation of the resulting cross-sections, and the scripts that produce every figure in the
paper.

## Reproducing everything

```bash
python3 make_geometry_figure.py          # figures/chg_coord.png
python3 make_paper_figures.py            # the four results figures, plus the numbers quoted in §6

python3 build_computing_M1.py
jupyter nbconvert --to notebook --execute --inplace computing_M1.ipynb

python3 build_notebook.py
jupyter nbconvert --to notebook --execute --inplace results_visualization.ipynb
```

`make_paper_figures.py` also prints, to stdout, every number quoted in Section 6 of the paper: the
SPM closure residual, the extremes of η, the polarization-ratio shift, the retrieval bias, and the
location of the coherence ridge.

## What `computing_M1.ipynb` does

The first-order geometric source is assembled from the metric perturbation by the double
Levi-Civita contraction of eq. (4.1),

```
M¹ₐ = (δₐᵦ G¹_de + G¹ₐᵦ δ_de) ε^bcd ε^efg ∂_c ∂_f E⁰_g
    + δₐᵦ ε^bcd (∂_c G¹_de) ε^efg ∂_f E⁰_g
```

with `G¹ₐᵦ = m¹ₐᵦ − γ_,ζ δₐᵦ`. The notebook evaluates this for TE and TM incidence, transforms the
result to momentum space, and specialises it to backscattering. The output is, in order:

* **TE** — eqs. (4.2), (4.3) and (4.4) of the paper, built on `Φ⁰` and its normal derivatives;
* **TM** — eqs. (4.6), (4.7) and (4.8);
* their **backscattering limit** (`pₓ = −k`, `p_y = 0`), which is the two-term integrand that
  Appendix A evaluates exactly, and from which `I_HH` and `I_VV` follow.

One feature of the result is worth pointing out. The TM sources depend on the zeroth-order field
**only** through the combination `Ψ⁰ = ∂_ζ E⁰_x − ∂_x E⁰_ζ`, which is proportional to the incident
magnetic field. That combination is an output of the contraction, not an assumption, and it is why
the auxiliary field of eq. (4.5) is the natural variable for the TM channel.

## What `results_visualization.ipynb` does

Implements eqs. (5.3) and (5.11) — the incoherent backscattered power in the HH and VV channels,
each a sum of a roughness term, a dielectric term and a cross term proportional to the
surface–dielectric correlation coefficient ρ₀ — and then:

1. **Checks the SPM closure** numerically. The geometric part of the VV amplitude equals the SPM
   kernel to ~10⁻¹⁵ for every angle and permittivity.
2. **Locates each mechanism** in incidence angle.
3. **Maps the departure from additivity** `η = I_cross/(I_rough + I_diel)` against angle and ρ₀.
4. **Exhibits the two conditions** for the coupling to be observable: one of balance between the
   two mechanisms, and one of coherence, `2L_v|K_t| ~ 1`, on the depth of the correlated layer.
5. **Quantifies the retrieval bias** incurred by inverting with an additive model.
6. **Reports a negative result**: the co-polarized ratio σ⁰_VV/σ⁰_HH is not a useful observable,
   because the coupling displaces both channels alike.

All quantities plotted are ratios of contributions *within one channel*, so none of them depends
on the overall normalisation convention adopted for σ⁰.

## Conventions

Two conventions matter for reading the code against the paper.

* **Fourier.** The factor `(2π)⁻²` sits on the forward transform,
  `a(p) = ∫ d²x/(2π)² e^{−ip·x} A(x)`, so that a convolution of two fields carries no extra factor
  and the spectral densities appear with none either:
  `⟨a(p) a*(p′)⟩ = W_a(p) δ(p − p′)`. This is the convention in which the roughness spectrum
  `W_h = s²l²e^{−p²l²/4}/(4π)` is written, and the one used in the remote-sensing literature the
  paper compares against.

* **Gauge.** The profile satisfies `f(0) = 1`, `f′(0) = 0` and `f → 0` at `ζ → ±∞`. The second
  condition makes the Jacobian equal to unity *on* the interface; within that class the first-order
  amplitudes are exactly independent of `f`. Profiles with `f′(0) ≠ 0` do not share that property,
  and `computing_M1.ipynb` and the appendix integrals both assume it.

Lengths in the figure scripts are expressed in free-space wavelengths.
